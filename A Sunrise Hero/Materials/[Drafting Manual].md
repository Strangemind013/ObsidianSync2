> **Drafting Manual – 120k Fantasy Novel**

---

# 📘 PART I — DAILY WRITING SYSTEM

## 🎯 Core Philosophy

- I am not writing beautifully.
    
- I am expanding my outline.
    
- Drafting = execution, not inspiration.
    
- Momentum > intensity.
    

---

## 🪜 The Tiered Writing System

Each writing day, choose your tier:

**🥉 Tier 1 – Bare Minimum**

- 500 words
    
- Protects momentum
    

**🥈 Tier 2 – Standard Day**

- 1,000 words
    
- Default target


**🥇 Tier 3 – Momentum Day**

- 1,500–2,000 words
    
- Only when energy is natural
    

Rule: No zero days.

---

## ⏱ Daily Drafting Structure

1. Read outline bullet for scene.
    
2. Write this sentence:
    
    > “In this scene, [Character] wants ___, but ___ gets in the way.”
    
3. Expand bullets into physical experience.
    
4. Stop without heavy editing.
    
5. Write tomorrow’s first paragraph before closing.
    

---

# 📘 PART II — WHAT 1,000 WORDS ACTUALLY LOOK LIKE

## 🧱 1,000 Words = Scene Expansion

A typical breakdown:

- 250–300 words: Setting & atmosphere
    
- 300–400 words: Internal thoughts
    
- 300–400 words: Dialogue + action
    

That’s it.

Not poetry.  
Not brilliance.  
Just movement.

---

## ⚙️ Drafting Standards (For Draft 1)

A scene succeeds if it:

- Moves plot forward
    
- Deepens character
    
- Raises tension
    
- Creates a new problem
    

Clunky prose is acceptable.  
Clear prose is enough.

---

## 🚫 Things Allowed in Draft 1

- On-the-nose emotion (“He was angry.”)
    
- Placeholder notes: `[add better insult later]`
    
- Basic description
    
- Blunt dialogue
    
- Repetition
    

Drafts are scaffolding.

---

# 📘 PART III — WRITING STRONG NEGOTIATION SCENES

## Every Scene Must Contain:

1. A goal
    
2. Resistance
    
3. Escalation
    
4. A shift in power
    

If power shifts, the scene works.

---

# 📘 PART IV — MAKING IRAGA (OR ANY ANTAGONIST) FEEL DANGEROUS

## 🔥 Rule 1: Dangerous People Don’t Perform Danger

Avoid:

- Shouting
    
- Slamming
    
- Over-description
    

Use:

- Stillness
    
- Controlled speech
    
- Deliberate silence
    

---

## 🧊 Rule 2: Let the Room React

Show danger through:

- Servants adjusting posture
    
- Guards straightening
    
- People speaking more carefully
    
- Pauses before answering
    

If others adjust, he has power.

---

## ⚔️ Rule 3: Underreaction > Overreaction

Instead of:

> “How dare you!”

Use:

> “You are very confident.”

Calm responses imply calculation.

---

## 🧠 Rule 4: Imply Leverage

He should:

- Know things he shouldn’t
    
- Reference hidden information
    
- Suggest consequences without stating them
    

Example:

> “You’re far from home.”

No threat stated.  
Threat understood.

---

## 🪓 Rule 5: Casual Authority

Small actions signal power:

- Dismisses someone without looking at them
    
- Interrupts and never apologizes
    
- Lets silence stretch
    

Stillness = control  
Control = danger

---

## ❌ Overwriting Test

If you use:

- dangerous
    
- terrifying
    
- intimidating
    
- powerful
    

Delete it.

Replace with behavior.

---

# 📘 PART V — ANTI-PROCRASTINATION RULES

## 1. The 10-Minute Rule

Write for 10 minutes.  
You may stop after.  
You rarely will.

## 2. If You Skip Two Days

Write 300 words before entertainment.

## 3. No Structural Edits Before Draft Completion

No re-outlining.  
No global pacing changes.  
No rebuilding magic system.

Execution only.

---

# 📘 PART VI — IDENTITY SHIFT

Instead of:

> “I need to finish my novel.”

Say:

> “I am someone who writes 5,000+ words per week.”

Books are built by identity, not bursts of motivation.
