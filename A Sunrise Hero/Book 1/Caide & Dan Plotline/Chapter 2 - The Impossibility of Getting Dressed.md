Like every other morning, Caide stared down the insurmountable challenge of getting dressed with one arm. Didn’t help that he barely got any sleep. Then again, when had he ever? He’d been warned long ago that ending lives takes away from your sleep, but of course he didn’t listen. Now he didn’t even have enough for a lengthy wink.

Every shirt he owned got two sleeves. He only needed one, and the other hung floppy at his side. He pushed a narrow bag of sand into the empty sleeve, held it fast, and worked the cloth around with his fingers to tie a clumsy knot. It helped with his balance.

Putting on trousers consists of a messy tangle of legs and fabric. He tried doing it seated, but the problem was made obvious once he worked up his thighs. Standing up undid all his progress. He considered wearing one of those work robes that merchants wore, but even that presented its own series of problems.

Belts, boots, and then the straps that held his sword (a prop). Each their own struggle. If only he could learn to bend his fingers backwards, that might’ve helped.

 If he had a washbasin, he could’ve checked himself out on the reflection. But no, handling a washbasin was a two-handed affair, so he had to settle for a bowl. He cupped his hand and scooped a pitiful amount of water and splashed away last night from his face. 
 
 He looked down at his unkempt self. Pitiful. No matter, he thought, the cloak will cover everything anyway. 

He put on the cloak, and headed out. There was a bump under his boot, but he couldn’t be bothered. Maybe later.

Nothing more disgusting than asking a friend for money. A different, specific kind of shame. It would’ve been easier if he was asking for help to hide the corpse of someone he just murdered. Or to confess a severe betrayal. Anything else, really. But to ask for money, and the thought of being rejected. The halfhearted excuse, the downward glance, the pity. He’d rather dive headfirst into the Crescent river.

Funny how being poor works. Quite similar to being infected by a putrid disease. Easier to blame someone for it, to think of it as a lack of wits, effort, or perseverance. If it was so easy, then no one would be poor, no? And yet Caide was poor, for all the might he possessed in his youth. He’d been focused on the wrong things. Honor, power, skill with the sword, sagecraft… He should’ve been amassing wealth instead. He should’ve turned to brigandry, his money problems would be solved in a few moons. But his last shreds of honor, damned honor, prevented him from doing so. It would’ve been so easy… But if he learned anything from his three decades of existence, it was that he wasn’t a man blessed enough to have it easy.
