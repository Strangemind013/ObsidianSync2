Like every other morning, Caide stared down the insurmountable challenge of getting dressed with one arm. Didn’t help that he barely got any sleep. Then again, when had he ever? Sleep was supposed to be one of the few pleasures he should be able to afford, bu

Every shirt he owned got two sleeves. He only needed one, and the other hung floppy at his side. He pushed a narrow bag of sand into the empty sleeve, held it fast, and worked the cloth around with his fingers to tie a clumsy knot. It helped with his balance.

Putting on trousers consists of a messy tangle of legs and fabric. He tried doing it seated, but the problem was made obvious once he worked up his thighs. Standing up undid all his progress. He considered wearing a skirt.

Belts, boots, and then the straps that held his sword (a prop). Each their own struggle.

 If he had a washbasin, he could’ve checked himself out on the reflection. But no, handling a washbasin is a two-handed affar, so he had to settle for a bowl. He cupped his hand and scooped a pitiful amount of water and splashed away his sleep. 
 
 He looked down at his unkempt self. Pitiful. No matter, he thought, the cloak will cover everything anyway. 

He put on the cloak, and headed out. There was a bump under his boot, but he couldn’t be bothered. Maybe later.
