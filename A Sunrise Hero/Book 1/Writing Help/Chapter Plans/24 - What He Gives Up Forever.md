**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 24 — What He Gives Up Forever  
**POV:** Caide — proposed change from the source's tentative Grin POV to show the irreversible choice directly.  
**Time and location:** Hira's home or its damaged surroundings; after Miero's injury.  
**Essential characters:** Caide, Hira, Grin, the gravely injured Miero.  
**Approximate word budget:** 4,200 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

#### Purpose
**Main contribution to the book:** Make Caide choose a useful imperfect life over the restoration he believed would make him whole.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** His acceptance arc would become a speech rather than a costly action.

#### Character motivation
**Immediate goal:** Protect whoever can still be helped and resist Grin.  
**Underlying motive:** At first prove restoration worked; ultimately act without needing to be restored.  
**Stakes:** Lives still at risk, Hira, Miero, and permanent loss of the prosthesis.

#### Dramatic question
**Question driving the chapter:** Will Caide keep the arm when keeping it means losing the ability he now needs?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** He knowingly gives it up forever, reclaiming sagecraft without defeating Grin.

#### Opposition and tension
**External resistance:** Grin is overwhelming; Hira's attack fails; the arm blocks Caide's magic.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Grin wants despair and may prolong Caide's suffering rather than kill him.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Caide must stop treating two hands as the condition of his worth.

#### Causal sequence
**Starting situation:** Hira confronts Grin over Miero's near-death state.  
**Trigger:** Their exchange hints at her experiments and past.  
**First approach:** Caide tries to intervene with the restored two-sword style.  
**Complication:** Hira dies and Caide discovers the arm has shut off his sagecraft.  
**Response:** He learns removal is permanent and chooses it to perform a specific necessary act.  
**Turning point:** Magic returns; Grin leaves him alive to struggle rather than granting a heroic death.

#### Decision and action
**Options the character perceives:** Keep the arm and forfeit sagecraft, or remove it with no future replacement.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Keeping preserves the desired body but prevents the necessary action; removal abandons the dream permanently.  
**Decision:** Remove it as an informed act of responsibility.  
**Action taken:** Discard the prosthesis himself or explicitly direct the irreversible procedure.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** His regained magic and reliance on others are tested immediately in Chapter 25.

#### Outcome and change
**Practical result:** Hira is dead; Caide has permanently surrendered the arm; Grin remains beyond him.  
**Before to after:** Restoration as condition of worth → action despite an accepted permanent loss.  
**Character interpretation:** He can begin accepting himself without being happy about every consequence.  
**Relationship effect:** Hira's loss and Miero's condition remain grief, not payment that neatly purchases growth.  
**Next consequence:** The Faceless encounter requires him to use his abilities and receive help.

#### Reader knowledge and continuity
**What the reader must understand:** The fitting secretly blocked magic; removal is one-time and cannot be reversed by another prosthesis.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Caide must understand enough before choosing for the sacrifice to be meaningful.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** Hira's full history can remain mysterious, but Grin's dialogue cannot supply random claims the story never intends to answer.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** Workshop, Miero's friendship, the long/short blades, and the arm's promised restoration.  
**Later event prepared:** Caide accepting Sol's rescue, teamwork, and the later apology.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Establish the causal lock between prosthesis and sagecraft and why another arm is impossible; no Break-plus-Endure combination.

#### Closing beat
**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Caide has lost the arm he wanted and still has something he must do.

#### One sentence summary
Because the prosthesis prevents the magic he needs in Grin's attack, Caide gives it up forever, losing his promised restoration while choosing to remain useful as he is.
