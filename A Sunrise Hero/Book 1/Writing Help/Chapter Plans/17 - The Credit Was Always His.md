**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 17 — The Credit Was Always His  
**POV:** Dan.  
**Time and location:** Cardinale's household or a court reception; Meroun is suffering while the crew trains.  
**Essential characters:** Dan, Cardinale, the princess, the king as needed.  
**Approximate word budget:** 3,000 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

#### Purpose
**Main contribution to the book:** Make Dan surrender the grain and expose how his father converts loyalty into leverage.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** Cardinale's later marriage reward would lack its material cause and personal betrayal.

#### Character motivation
**Immediate goal:** Release the grain in a way that helps Meroun and restores his place.  
**Underlying motive:** Keep believing his service can earn recognition as Cardinale's son.  
**Stakes:** His reserve, his secret relationship, and his remaining hope of belonging.

#### Dramatic question
**Question driving the chapter:** Will giving Cardinale the success Dan always intended for him repair their relationship?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** No; Cardinale accepts the benefit while retaining control over recognition and the princess's secret.

#### Opposition and tension
**External resistance:** Cardinale recognizes Dan's lover as the king's older sister and implies he can protect or expose the secret.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Obtain the grain and retain control of Dan.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Dan wants to defend himself but still interprets compliance as loyalty.

#### Causal sequence
**Starting situation:** The shortage makes Dan's stored grain politically valuable.  
**Trigger:** Cardinale recognizes the princess from Snowdrop Garden.  
**First approach:** Dan argues for using the grain to relieve the city.  
**Complication:** His father links personal safety and standing to obedience, explicitly or by implication.  
**Response:** Dan transfers control while insisting that public credit was always intended for Cardinale.  
**Turning point:** He leaves with nothing equivalent granted in return.

#### Decision and action
**Options the character perceives:** Withhold the grain, release it independently, or hand it to his father.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Withholding hurts hungry people; independent release invites retaliation; surrender deepens dependence.  
**Decision:** Give up control, while beginning to register the pattern.  
**Action taken:** Authorize the transfer and privately revisit his pre-existing heir declaration.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** The reward for the grain becomes public in Chapter 20.

#### Outcome and change
**Practical result:** Cardinale gains the resource that will earn him royal favor.  
**Before to after:** Private reserve and hope of bargaining → reduced leverage and growing resentment.  
**Character interpretation:** Dan can still call the sacrifice his own plan, but that explanation no longer comforts him.  
**Relationship effect:** His father now knows the relationship Dan most wants protected.  
**Next consequence:** The marriage announcement will turn this concession against Dan.

#### Reader knowledge and continuity
**What the reader must understand:** The woman is the king's older sister; the royal reveal belongs here if not earlier.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Dan knows the declaration is forged and that Cardinale has withheld genuine recognition.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** He may keep the document secret from others, but his POV should not imply it is authentic.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** The grain investment, secret romance, and dependence on approval.  
**Later event prepared:** The marriage reward and later legitimization after Cardinale's death.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** House of Works is not the grain ministry; distinguish private ownership, relief distribution, and royal credit.

#### Closing beat
**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Dan notices that the grain has changed hands, but the promise of sonship has not moved any closer.

#### One sentence summary
Because the shortage makes his grain valuable, Dan gives Cardinale the public success he always intended, but his father's knowledge of the princess turns loyalty into another means of control.
