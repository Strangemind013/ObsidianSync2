**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 20 — The Reward and the Bargain  
**POV:** Dan.  
**Time and location:** Royal reward audience, then the prison; before the wedding and following-week execution.  
**Essential characters:** Dan, Caide, Cardinale, the king, the princess, Grin; relevant custodians.  
**Approximate word budget:** 4,000 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

#### Purpose
**Main contribution to the book:** Break Dan's belief that loyalty can earn sonship and turn him into an active conspirator.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The later riots, assassination, and forged succession would lack a clear personal decision.

#### Character motivation
**Immediate goal:** Initially secure recognition and the crew's release; later prevent the marriage and remove Cardinale.  
**Underlying motive:** Stop living on terms that can never make him a son.  
**Stakes:** The princess, the prisoners, the city, and Dan's moral responsibility.

#### Dramatic question
**Question driving the chapter:** What will Dan do once he sees that no amount of service will earn the place he wants?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** He chooses power and revenge through a bargain that endangers the city.

#### Opposition and tension
**External resistance:** Caide selects jade instead of immediate pardons; the king rewards Cardinale with the princess; Grin demands valuable information.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Cardinale wants the marriage and control; Caide wants restoration; Grin wants maximum despair.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Dan can accurately identify abuse while using that truth to excuse wider harm.

#### Causal sequence
**Starting situation:** The victory produces a reward choice.  
**Trigger:** Caide takes the jade, still intending to find pardons later.  
**First approach:** Dan attempts to preserve the remaining benefits of success.  
**Complication:** The marriage is announced only after Caide's choice, and Cardinale's belief about noble birth destroys Dan's remaining hope.  
**Response:** Dan learns the tortured Grin is already beyond their capacity to contain and seeks an audience.  
**Turning point:** He trades the information that Tahan's death would devastate Meroun for Grin killing both Tahan and Cardinale.

#### Decision and action
**Options the character perceives:** Accept the marriage, resist without Grin, or exploit an escape he cannot prevent.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Submission loses the princess and perpetuates control; independent resistance is uncertain; the bargain knowingly sacrifices a beloved protector.  
**Decision:** Choose the bargain and take ownership of it.  
**Action taken:** Give Grin actionable despair-producing intelligence and agree the targets.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** The staged rescue buys time in Chapter 21; Grin acts in Chapter 22.

#### Outcome and change
**Practical result:** Dan abandons reconciliation and becomes complicit in the coming catastrophe.  
**Before to after:** Son seeking approval → claimant choosing his own power through lethal means.  
**Character interpretation:** He tells himself removing abusers will let him do better; the Tahan bargain tests that justification.  
**Relationship effect:** Trust with his father ends; Caide's priorities also wound him.  
**Next consequence:** The wedding must be delayed until Grin can kill Cardinale.

#### Reader knowledge and continuity
**What the reader must understand:** Caide has no pardon solution when he chooses jade; the engagement is announced afterward. Dan and Caide know escape is inevitable, but Caide need not know the assassination bargain.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Dan knows his declaration is forged and Grin's deal harms more than his father.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** Do not hide Dan's actual bargain through evasive inner narration. Princess ignorance is separate.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** The grain transfer, withheld heirship, Caide's insecurity, and forged contingency.  
**Later event prepared:** The riots, execution targets, loss of public hope, and pragmatic succession.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Pain fuels Grin's magic; despair funds revival of his lover. These are different mechanisms.

#### Closing beat
**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Dan supplies the name of the person whose death will hurt Meroun most.

#### One sentence summary
Because Cardinale's marriage reward proves Dan can never earn sonship, Dan exploits Grin's inevitable escape by trading the city's greatest hope for the deaths that will open his own path to power.
