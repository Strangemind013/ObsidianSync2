**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 25 — Three Nudges

**POV:** Caide.

**Time and location:** A ruined street near the previous confrontation; immediately afterward.

**Essential characters:** Caide, the Faceless, Grin briefly, Sol, his companions, the Smokesons; Seyla at the end.

**Approximate word budget:** 3,300 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

## Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Make accepting rescue an active part of Caide's changed behavior and reunite the trained team.

**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The later collective victory would begin as another attempt at a solo heroic finish.

## Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Survive the Faceless and keep it from reaching others.

**Underlying motive:** Use what remains of his abilities without claiming he can do everything alone.

**Stakes:** His life and everyone within reach of the creature.

## Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Can Caide defeat an opponent that regenerates and learns his fighting style?

**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** Not alone; he survives by trusting a signal and moving for the team's shot.

## Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** The Faceless regenerates and adapts; physical damage does not last.

**What the opposing character wants:** Its precise purpose is not established; on page it attacks Caide.

**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Caide can mistake receiving help for another proof of inadequacy.

## Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** Caide readies his regained magic against Grin.

**Trigger:** Grin redirects him toward the arriving Faceless.

**First approach:** Caide fights with skill and Break.

**Complication:** The creature adapts, regenerates, and catches him by the throat.

**Response:** Three nudges from Sol's coded studs tell him to move; he cuts free and rolls.

**Turning point:** The ballista shot and arriving allies convert imminent death into coordinated resistance.

## Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Keep trying to finish alone or obey a signal whose whole plan he cannot see.

**Cost or risk of each option:** Solo resistance is failing; trust requires surrendering control of the next moment.

**Decision:** Move when signaled.

**Action taken:** Sever the creature's gripping arm, clear the shot, and let the crew act.

**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** The completed restraint and Seyla's diagnosis follow in Chapter 26.

## Outcome and change

**Practical result:** Caide survives; the group gains a working position, but cannot yet destroy the creature.

**Before to after:** Isolated fighter → rescued participant in a shared plan.

**Character interpretation:** He experiences others' competence as something he can use rather than erase.

**Relationship effect:** The crew returns for him despite its complicated history with his reward choice.

**Next consequence:** Seyla brings knowledge that changes the objective from wounding to overwhelming regeneration.

## Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** The team performs the practiced maneuver competently; regeneration is the new problem.

**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Caide knows the three-nudge code because it was established in training.

**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** The Faceless's origin can remain beyond his knowledge, but its immediate behavior must be legible.

**Earlier setup paid off:** Sol's stud signals, the ballista, and the crew's assigned roles.

**Later event prepared:** The clean seal restraint, need for light, and Caide's coordinated flash.

**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Physical bolts and external restraints affect a living target indirectly; Caide does not magically alter its body.

## Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Caide is alive because he moved for someone else's shot.

## One sentence summary

Because the Faceless adapts to his solo attacks, Caide is nearly killed, but he trusts Sol's coded signal and lets the returning crew turn a rescue into a coordinated fight.
