**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 23 — The Arm Cannot Make Him Enough  
**POV:** Caide.  
**Time and location:** Dan's refuge and Hira's neighborhood; during or immediately after the execution disaster.  
**Essential characters:** Caide, Dan, Miero; Hira or witnesses as needed.  
**Approximate word budget:** 2,600 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

#### Purpose
**Main contribution to the book:** Test Caide's restored body against a crisis it cannot solve and bring him into the personal loss at Hira's home.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The arm sacrifice would follow a fitting rather than a demonstrated failure of its promise.

#### Character motivation
**Immediate goal:** Find Dan, then reach Miero before Grin's violence takes him.  
**Underlying motive:** Act as the protector he believes the new arm has restored.  
**Stakes:** His friends' lives and the fantasy that restoration guarantees control.

#### Dramatic question
**Question driving the chapter:** Does having two hands let Caide prevent the losses he fears?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** No; he cannot make Dan whole or reach Miero in time.

#### Opposition and tension
**External resistance:** Citywide chaos, Dan's withdrawal, and the speed of the attack.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Dan wants escape from what he has done; Grin wants suffering and despair.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Caide wants to treat every emergency as proof he can now be sufficient.

#### Causal sequence
**Starting situation:** Caide has obtained and fitted the prosthesis; exactly when must be established.  
**Trigger:** He finds Dan drunk amid the catastrophe.  
**First approach:** He tries to make Dan act or explain.  
**Complication:** News or direct evidence puts Miero in immediate danger.  
**Response:** Caide leaves or delegates Dan's care and tries to reach the child.  
**Turning point:** Miero is gravely hurt despite the attempt, leading into Hira's confrontation.

#### Decision and action
**Options the character perceives:** Stay with Dan or respond to Miero's danger.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Staying may miss the child's rescue; leaving abandons a distressed friend unless someone else can help.  
**Decision:** Proposed: get Dan into another person's care and go.  
**Action taken:** Attempt a specific rescue whose failure is physically legible.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** Hira's response and the arm's full cost are addressed in Chapter 24.

#### Outcome and change
**Practical result:** Caide reaches the consequence rather than preventing it.  
**Before to after:** Restored body as reassurance → restored body unable to guarantee protection.  
**Character interpretation:** He risks blaming himself more deeply before he understands the arm's magical cost.  
**Relationship effect:** Dan's condition strains the friendship; Miero's injury makes the loss intimate.  
**Next consequence:** Hira confronts Grin and Caide must choose how to respond.
