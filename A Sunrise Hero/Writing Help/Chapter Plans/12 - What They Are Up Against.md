**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 12 — What They Are Up Against

**POV:** Pao — proposed choice from the original Smokeson/Dan options.

**Time and location:** Fool's Mug and, briefly, the surrounding hungry streets; after the defeat.

**Essential characters:** Pao, Caide, Dan, the Smokesons.

**Approximate word budget:** 2,500 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

## Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Turn the defeat into a focused request for help and establish only the magic rules needed for the next plan.

**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The alliance with Sol and prisoner training would feel like arbitrary escalation.

## Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Pao: learn whether there is any survivable way to continue.

**Underlying motive:** Keep the prospect of freedom from becoming another false promise.

**Stakes:** Life, pardons, and the people still living under the fires.

## Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Is there a way forward that does not require Caide to beat Grin alone?

**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** Yes: outside expertise, suppression, and testing the crew for Sage potential.

## Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** Fear, injuries, and the taboo against commoner sagecraft.

**What the opposing character wants:** Some crew members want survival; Dan wants a workable operation; Caide resists exposing more of himself.

**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Pao can want reassurance while distrusting heroic promises after the defeat.

## Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** The team is grounded while the city's situation worsens.

**Trigger:** Someone asks why Caide's magic was not enough.

**First approach:** Caide explains the relevant limitations through the failed fight.

**Complication:** Learning sagecraft is itself politically dangerous and not everyone will be capable.

**Response:** Pao asks what non-Sages can do; Dan considers who could supply the missing power.

**Turning point:** They agree to approach Tahan Iraga rather than resume suicidal responses.

## Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Disband, continue unchanged, or seek help and retrain.

**Cost or risk of each option:** Disbanding loses the route to freedom; continuing invites death; asking help creates dependence and exposure.

**Decision:** Proposed: Pao stays on a conditional, practical basis.

**Action taken:** Demand a role and a plan instead of another promise of heroism.

**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** Dan and Caide seek Tahan in Chapter 13.

## Outcome and change

**Practical result:** The crew has a next step, with its fears acknowledged.

**Before to after:** Defeated and uninformed → frightened but able to name what is missing.

**Character interpretation:** Pao sees that asking useful questions can affect the plan.

**Relationship effect:** Caide begins being accountable to men he initially expected simply to command.

**Next consequence:** Sol's involvement and the training become necessary responses.

## Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** Commoner Sages are suppressed, not universally extinct; noble families retain them.

**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Pao knows the risks being explained and may misunderstand magic as automatic superiority.

**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** Grin's capture plan and pain-powered escape remain genuinely unknown here.

**Earlier setup paid off:** The defeat, prison bargain, and crew's uneven skills.

**Later event prepared:** Testing Hiyang, meaningful non-Sage roles, and the request for suppression expertise.

**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Inscriptions; Hiyang; maximum three combined Words; no direct alteration of another living body; external effects remain dangerous.

## One sentence summary

Because Grin has stopped the crew from working, Pao presses for a survivable plan, and Caide's explanation leads Dan to seek the suppression expertise they lack.
