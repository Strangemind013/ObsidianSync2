**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 8 — Permission to Break Houses  
**POV:** Dan.  
**Time and location:** Fool's Mug, a religious official's receiving room, and a merchant's premises.  
**Essential characters:** Dan, Caide, a prominent religious figure, a merchant or supplier.  
**Approximate word budget:** 3,300 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

### Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Show Dan making Caide's spontaneous firebreak response politically and materially possible.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The crew would have no answer to religious objection, destroyed homes, or basic equipment needs.

### Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Obtain toleration, equipment, and a credible compensation arrangement.  
**Underlying motive:** Redeem himself through his father's assignment and protect the crew's ability to work.  
**Stakes:** The next fire response, ruined households, and Dan's remaining credibility.

### Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Can Dan make a condemned activity viable after losing borrowed authority?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** Yes, through a narrow religious distinction and a hard-won funding arrangement.

### Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** A religious figure condemns resisting sacred fires; a merchant knows Dan has been demoted.  
**What the opposing character wants:** The religious figure wants orthodoxy preserved; the merchant wants profit and protection from liability.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Dan is tempted to treat future inheritance as already secure.

### Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** Caide proposes demolishing whichever next house can interrupt a fire's spread.  
**Trigger:** Dan realizes success will produce victims and accusations.  
**First approach:** He argues that they move houses, not the Summer God's fire.  
**Complication:** Toleration still leaves them unable to equip men or compensate owners.  
**Response:** He pressures a merchant using known irregularities; the merchant points to Dan's own signatures.  
**Turning point:** Dan shifts to mutual benefit, Cardinale's public credit, and a capped written commitment.

### Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Proceed without safeguards, ask his father, or negotiate obligations himself.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Improvisation abandons householders; asking invites refusal; negotiation exposes Dan's compromised leverage.  
**Decision:** Arrange resources now and accept responsibility for the promise.  
**Action taken:** Secure tools, coats, immediate lodging, and rebuilding labor/material vouchers.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** Actual demolition and public reaction occur in Chapter 9.

### Outcome and change

**Practical result:** A bounded support arrangement exists; Caide can choose a firebreak on arrival.  
**Before to after:** Helpless demoted agent → effective organizer without restored rank.  
**Character interpretation:** Dan sees this as proof that his father's test can be passed.  
**Relationship effect:** Caide receives practical backing; merchants gain leverage over Dan too.  
**Next consequence:** The first demolition will test whether these promises mean anything.

### Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** No houses are selected in advance; funds cover houses destroyed in an actual response.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Dan knows compensation cannot undo losing a home.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** The deal should be legible to readers; no concealed unlimited funding solution.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** Chapter 1's administrative skill now works without reliable authority.  
**Later event prepared:** Displaced residents, public goodwill, and Cardinale benefiting from Dan's initiative.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Tools: hooks, ropes, axes, sledges, carts. Coats require a chosen material and stable inscriptions; Caide reinforces them secretly.

### Scene list

1. **Location:** Base | **Objective:** Turn Caide's idea into requirements | **Resistance:** Religious and financial obstacles | **Outcome:** Dan identifies two necessary agreements.
2. **Location:** Religious receiving room | **Objective:** Win toleration | **Resistance:** Sacred-fire objection | **Outcome:** A narrow demolition exception is conceded.
3. **Location:** Merchant's premises | **Objective:** Secure practical support | **Resistance:** Counter-blackmail and limited credit | **Outcome:** A written, capped package includes shelter as well as rebuilding.

### Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Dan leaves carrying a commitment he must honor, still pleased that his father will receive the public credit.

### One sentence summary

Because Caide can stop spread by demolishing houses, Dan negotiates religious toleration and pressures a merchant into funding equipment and compensation, taking responsibility for promises his rank no longer guarantees.
