**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 21 — The Interrupted Procession  
**POV:** The princess; her personal name remains open.  
**Time and location:** Meroun's wedding procession, a secluded refuge, and the royal return.  
**Essential characters:** Princess, Caide, Cardinale, the king, rioters and attendants; Dan through his hidden arrangement.  
**Approximate word budget:** 3,300 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

### Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Show the human experience of Dan's manufactured crisis and pay off Caide's unresolved pardon intention.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The delayed marriage and sudden pardons would become convenient off-page outcomes.

### Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** The princess: get through a wedding she does not want and understand whether Dan will intervene.  
**Underlying motive:** Preserve her bond with Dan and some control over her future.  
**Stakes:** Her safety, bodily autonomy, and marriage.

### Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Will the procession deliver her to Cardinale, or can she escape the marriage?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** The wedding is delayed, but the apparent rescue contains violence she does not understand.

### Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** An arranged public marriage and a riot interrupt her movement.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Cardinale wants the marriage completed; Caide wants the staged rescue to work and a reward for the crew.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** She trusts Dan to prevent the marriage but lacks knowledge of his means.

### Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** She enters the procession expecting an unwanted ceremony.  
**Trigger:** Riots break its route and security.  
**First approach:** She tries to determine whom to trust.  
**Complication:** Caide takes her away and inflicts a mild side wound as part of the staging.  
**Response:** He returns her publicly as a rescued victim.  
**Turning point:** The king grants a rescue reward; Caide asks for the crew's pardons and the marriage is postponed.

### Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Resist Caide, cooperate on trust, or seek another escort.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Resistance may return her to the riot; trust exposes her to a plan she has not consented to.  
**Decision:** Her exact response is open; do not assume informed agreement.  
**Action taken:** Show her observable choice and reaction to the injury.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** Recovery, seasonal postponement, and the execution follow.

### Outcome and change

**Practical result:** The marriage cannot happen before the next permitted season; Caide secures pardons through the apparent rescue.  
**Before to after:** Bride being delivered → injured royal returned under protection.  
**Character interpretation:** She may feel relief, fear, and betrayal together; no prescribed forgiveness.  
**Relationship effect:** Dan's love now has methods that can damage her trust; Caide fulfills the crew's practical hope.  
**Next consequence:** Grin has the following week to kill Cardinale.

**What the reader must understand:** Latest baseline: she is not in on the riot or staged rescue. This supersedes the earlier broader suggestion of agreement.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** She can identify Caide without knowing the conspiracy.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** The organization stays offstage because of her POV, but earlier planning must make it plausible; do not falsely present a known captor as unknown in her thoughts.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** Caide's intention to seek pardons; the announced marriage.
