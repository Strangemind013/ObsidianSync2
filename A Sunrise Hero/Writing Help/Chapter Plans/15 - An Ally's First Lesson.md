**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 15 — An Ally's First Lesson  
**POV:** Sol.  
**Time and location:** The Smokesons' base and a training yard; after the Iraga agreement.  
**Essential characters:** Sol, his three companions, Caide, Dan, the Smokesons.  
**Approximate word budget:** 2,800 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

### Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Let Sol assess the crew as people and establish what his accomplished team still has to learn from Caide.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The alliance would proceed straight to training without mutual adjustment or demonstrated expertise.

### Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Assess capabilities and make the mission trainable.  
**Underlying motive:** Carry out his responsibility competently while protecting his companions.  
**Stakes:** A flawed assessment could kill the crew during the Grin confrontation.

### Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Can Sol turn this uneven group into usable partners?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** He identifies promising Sages and learns that superior resources do not replace battlefield reading.

### Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** Poor equipment, mistrust, uneven aptitude, and Caide's experience.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Caide wants the new allies to respect him and his crew.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Sol must separate justified confidence from assumptions attached to noble training.

### Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** Sol arrives and sees the crew's living and working conditions.  
**Trigger:** An assessment exposes their uneven knowledge and equipment.  
**First approach:** He tests potential and proposes a controlled exercise.  
**Complication:** Caide exploits a pattern in Sol's coordinated approach.  
**Response:** Sol adjusts, but Caide wins through a specific feint.  
**Turning point:** Sol treats the result as information and begins planning training.

### Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Dismiss the group, make the exercise a status contest, or revise his assessment.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Dismissal wastes allies; rivalry breeds unsafe lessons; revision requires acknowledging a blind spot.  
**Decision:** Respect Caide's expertise without surrendering his own.  
**Action taken:** Identify Batch and Harum's potential and agree on complementary instruction.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** The human cost and practical work of learning occupy Chapter 16.

### Outcome and change

**Practical result:** The alliance has a shared baseline.  
**Before to after:** Unfamiliar poor recruits → individuals with different capabilities.  
**Character interpretation:** Sol recognizes value that his initial inspection could not measure.  
**Relationship effect:** Caide gains respect, but his need to win can remain visible.  
**Next consequence:** Training can assign Sage and non-Sage jobs deliberately.

### Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** Sol is accomplished; he loses this exercise to a veteran's feint, not basic incompetence.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** He sees poverty at close range without needing to be entirely ignorant of its existence.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** Unknown prisoner potential is tested honestly; not all seven become Sages.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** The duel and the crew's practical needs.  
**Later event prepared:** Harum's protection, Batch's Break, non-Sage support, and seal choreography.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Stable inscriptions, Hiyang differences, and Sol's seals require clearly defined preparation and range.

### Scene list

### Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Sol starts assigning complementary tasks rather than sorting the men into valuable Sages and everyone else.

### One sentence summary

Because Sol must prepare unfamiliar allies to face Grin, he tests the crew and spars with Caide, whose feint exposes a tactical blind spot and helps define the training they need.
