**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 9 — The House They Have to Lose  
**POV:** Duck — proposed single POV instead of the original Caide/Duck split.  
**Time and location:** An actively burning neighborhood; immediately following the new preparations.  
**Essential characters:** Duck, Caide, the Smokesons, the owners of the demolished house.  
**Approximate word budget:** 3,300 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

#### Purpose
**Main contribution to the book:** Pay off the firebreak and show that success has identifiable victims and a personal cost.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The technique would remain theoretical and Duck's later peg leg would arrive without context.

#### Character motivation
**Immediate goal:** Duck: help stop the spread and get endangered people out.  
**Underlying motive:** Remain useful to the crew while protecting people he can recognize as neighbors.  
**Stakes:** Residents' lives, a family's home, and Duck's own safety.

#### Dramatic question
**Question driving the chapter:** Can destroying one house save the rest of the row?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** Yes, but the lost home and Duck's injury prevent a clean celebration.

#### Opposition and tension
**External resistance:** The next viable firebreak is somebody's home; its occupants resist; the fire keeps advancing.  
**What the opposing character wants:** The household wants its possessions and security preserved.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Duck wants to help everyone and may stay too long.

#### Causal sequence
**Starting situation:** The crew reaches a fire water cannot stop.  
**Trigger:** Caide identifies the next house as the usable gap.  
**First approach:** They try to clear it and explain the demolition.  
**Complication:** Resistance or a remaining person delays the work; Duck becomes trapped or injured.  
**Response:** Caide uses sagecraft to reach or extract him while the crew completes the demolition.  
**Turning point:** The fire reaches the gap and fails to cross.

#### Decision and action
**Options the character perceives:** Duck can obey withdrawal or continue one necessary rescue.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Withdrawal may leave someone behind; delay may cost his life.  
**Decision:** Proposed: finish the rescue, then accept Caide's help when he cannot escape.  
**Action taken:** Get the person clear and signal rather than hide his condition.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** Recovery and the peg leg are addressed in Chapter 10.

#### Outcome and change
**Practical result:** The firebreak works; Duck survives, injured; one household loses its home.  
**Before to after:** Failed firefighters → a crew with a demonstrated method.  
**Character interpretation:** Duck can take pride without pretending the price was small.  
**Relationship effect:** Caide makes a teammate's life more important than hiding every ability.  
**Next consequence:** Compensation is demanded; public support begins.

#### Reader knowledge and continuity
**What the reader must understand:** Destroying the selected house interrupts spread; it does not extinguish Grin's power.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Duck sees what Caide does but may not understand all of it.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** Caide's Sage identity is no longer secret from Dan; only the crew's knowledge remains to decide.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** The failed water response, gear, and compensation agreement.  
**Later event prepared:** Duck's peg leg, the crew's reputation, and Grin noticing them.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Show exactly which Word allows the rescue without combining Break and Endure.

#### Scene list
1. **Location:** Firefront | **Objective:** Create the emergency gap | **Resistance:** Owners resist and time runs short | **Outcome:** Evacuation and demolition begin.
2. **Location:** Endangered house | **Objective:** Get Duck and any remaining resident out | **Resistance:** Injury and structural danger | **Outcome:** Caide intervenes; the crew completes the gap.
3. **Location:** Firebreak edge | **Objective:** See whether it worked | **Resistance:** Angry owners remain | **Outcome:** The row survives; compensation obligations become real.

#### Closing beat
**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Ash falls over the surviving street while a displaced family looks at the empty space where its home stood.

#### One sentence summary
Because a spreading fire threatens a row of homes, Duck helps the crew demolish the next house, but a rescue costs him an injury even as their first firebreak succeeds.
