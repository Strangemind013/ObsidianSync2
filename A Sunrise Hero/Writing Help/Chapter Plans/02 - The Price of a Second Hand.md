**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 2 — The Price of a Second Hand  
**POV:** Caide.  
**Time and location:** Caide's room, then Meroun's streets; shortly after Chapter 1.  
**Essential characters:** Caide, Dan, the assailants Caide hires.  
**Approximate word budget:** 3,400 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

#### Purpose
**Main contribution to the book:** Make the prosthesis emotionally necessary to Caide and make his first betrayal an accountable choice.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** Dan's lost money and Caide's later apology would lack their cause.

### Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Obtain the three thousand marks needed for a magical prosthesis.  
**Underlying motive:** Recover everyday independence and the swordsmanship through which he recognizes himself.  
**Stakes:** His friendship, Dan's safety and finances, and his secret identity as a Sage.

### Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** How far will Caide go when a direct request fails?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** He manufactures danger and makes Dan pay for his protection.

### Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** Dan refuses a loan he cannot comfortably afford.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Protect funds already committed to his own plans.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Caide experiences asking for help as humiliation and prefers coercion disguised as usefulness.

### Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** Dressing and preparing one-handed reinforce Caide's frustration.  
**Trigger:** Dan declines the request for money.  
**First approach:** Caide argues the prosthesis will restore his value.  
**Complication:** The arranged assailants confront them, creating real physical danger.  
**Response:** Caide offers rescue in exchange for funding and reveals his magic.  
**Turning point:** He narrowly wins; Dan agrees to finance the arm.

### Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Accept refusal, seek another route, or exploit the staged attack.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Waiting delays the arm; coercion endangers Dan and damages trust.  
**Decision:** Use the attack to force agreement.  
**Action taken:** Fight with his current limitations rather than his remembered abilities.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** The emotional reckoning continues in Chapter 4.

### Outcome and change

**Practical result:** Caide obtains funding; Dan loses his remaining accessible cash.  
**Before to after:** Ashamed petitioner → financially successful betrayer.  
**Character interpretation:** Caide can tell himself that proving his value justified the means.  
**Relationship effect:** Dan gains a powerful Sage and loses grounds for uncomplicated trust.  
**Next consequence:** The payment contributes to Dan's vulnerability after demotion.

### Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** Caide arranged this voluntarily; no blackmailer or stolen ledger forces him.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** He believes restored physical capability will restore his worth.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** Dan need not learn every arrangement immediately, but Caide's POV must not falsely deny his own plan.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** The prologue's mastery contrasts with this scrappy victory.  
**Later event prepared:** The apology, jade shipment, Dan's interest in a Sage, and the ultimate arm sacrifice.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Caide has Break and Endure Hiyang; the blade's destructive edge is Break. Commoner Sages face persecution.

### Scene list

1. **Location:** Caide's room | **Objective:** Get dressed and leave independently | **Resistance:** Ordinary tasks magnify frustration | **Outcome:** He commits to asking Dan.
2. **Location:** Street encounter | **Objective:** Secure the money | **Resistance:** Dan refuses; hired attackers create danger | **Outcome:** Caide coerces funding and barely wins.

### Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Dan imagines what Caide could do with both hands; Caide takes that hope as validation.

### One sentence summary

Because he believes a prosthesis will restore his worth, Caide turns Dan's refusal into a staged rescue bargain, obtaining the money at the cost of their trust.
