**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 7 — Water Against the Summer Fires  
**POV:** Batch — proposed reassignment from Caide to give him a prisoner chapter.  
**Time and location:** Spinner's Row and the crew's base; the first fire response.  
**Essential characters:** Batch, Caide, the seven Smokesons, affected residents; Hira/Miero only if needed.  
**Approximate word budget:** 3,300 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

### Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Make the first method fail, expose the crew's mistrust, and force Caide into actual leadership.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The firebreak would solve an untested problem and the later training would lack a starting failure.

### Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Batch: survive the assignment without letting Caide treat him as disposable.  
**Underlying motive:** Preserve dignity and resist another authority claiming ownership of him.  
**Stakes:** His life, the crew's safety, and any chance of earning release.

### Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Can this reluctant crew stop a summer fire with water?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** No, but the failure shows they need a different method rather than more obedience.

### Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** The fire overwhelms their equipment; Caide and Batch clash.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Caide wants coordinated compliance; the fire has no negotiable motive.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Batch wants to prove the leader wrong while also needing him to be competent.

### Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** The crew arrives with water and improvised equipment.  
**Trigger:** Flames spread faster than their response.  
**First approach:** Caide orders an ordinary suppression effort.  
**Complication:** The water fails and personal friction disrupts coordination.  
**Response:** Batch follows a concrete rescue or withdrawal order when he sees its purpose.  
**Turning point:** The crew escapes the failed response; Caide begins identifying spread, not flame, as the problem.

### Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Keep fighting for pride, refuse orders, or coordinate withdrawal.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Staying risks lives; refusal endangers teammates; cooperation feels like submission.  
**Decision:** Batch cooperates on a specific, justified task.  
**Action taken:** Help extract people or equipment under Caide's direction.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** Caide's proposed firebreak is presented to Dan in Chapter 8.

### Outcome and change

**Practical result:** People survive, but houses burn and the crew cannot claim it stopped the fire.  
**Before to after:** Unproven confidence → a shared failure requiring a new approach.  
**Character interpretation:** Batch sees Caide's limitations, with the first evidence that he will not simply abandon the men.  
**Relationship effect:** Hostility becomes grudging attention, not instant friendship.  
**Next consequence:** Caide must design an approach the crew can actually execute.

### Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** They do not yet know Grin is causing the fires.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Batch cannot narrate Caide's private realization; show questions, observation, or a brief spoken insight.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** The larger cause remains unknown because no one has encountered Grin.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** The recruitment's promises and threats.  
**Later event prepared:** The firebreak idea, Batch's destructive aptitude, Duck's family concern if retained.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Water's failure needs a consistent physical or magical reason; do not establish unlimited fire immunity.

### Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Caide studies the next unburned house instead of the flames; Batch cannot yet tell whether this is insight or another bad idea.

### One sentence summary

Because the crew's first water response fails, Batch challenges Caide's competence, but a necessary coordinated withdrawal gives them a reason to seek a different method together.
