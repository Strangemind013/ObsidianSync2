**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 29 — A Hand Offered

**POV:** Caide.

**Time and location:** A quiet refuge after the main crisis; location and elapsed time open.

**Essential characters:** Caide and his seven Smokeson friends; others only if necessary.

**Approximate word budget:** 2,200 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

## Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Show Caide living differently after the sacrifice, including accountability for the reward choice.

**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** His acceptance would stop at battlefield behavior and never reach ordinary relationships.

## Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Apologize without bargaining for forgiveness and accept help with an ordinary task.

**Underlying motive:** Remain among these men as a friend rather than justify belonging through legendary usefulness.

**Stakes:** Trust, the future of the group, and Caide's ability to live with his changed body.

## Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Can Caide admit that he put restoration ahead of the men's certainty of freedom without making himself the victim?

**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** Proposed: yes, by naming the risk he imposed and allowing individual responses.

## Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** The men can be grateful for their pardons and still angry about the initial choice.

**What the opposing character wants:** They want acknowledgment, honesty, and perhaps different futures after freedom.

**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Caide wants reassurance but must not make the apology another request for them to restore his self-worth.

## Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** The immediate danger has passed enough for conversation.

**Trigger:** The reward choice or their coming separation can no longer be avoided.

**First approach:** Caide starts to explain why he chose jade.

**Complication:** He recognizes that his motives do not cancel what he risked for them.

**Response:** He bows before all seven and apologizes directly.

**Turning point:** Proposed: later, he accepts a practical hand mounting a horse rather than insisting on managing alone.

## Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Defend the outcome, withdraw in shame, or own the choice.

**Cost or risk of each option:** Defense dismisses their risk; withdrawal repeats abandonment; honesty gives them room to judge him.

**Decision:** Apologize and remain available without demanding absolution.

**Action taken:** Name what he did, listen, and accept one concrete offer of help.

**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** His future can remain open without another magical restoration promise.

## Outcome and change

**Practical result:** An honest reckoning occurs; reconciliation may be partial rather than unanimous.

**Before to after:** Man proving he deserves company → man able to receive company and assistance.

**Character interpretation:** He need not stop grieving the arm to recognize himself as a whole person.

**Relationship effect:** The crew can remain friends without becoming a chorus of instant forgiveness.

**Next consequence:** Caide can choose a continuing purpose that includes other people.

## Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** The pardons were actually secured in Chapter 21. He apologizes for risking them and prioritizing jade, not for permanently abandoning the men.

**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Caide knows his own motive and should acknowledge it plainly.

**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** How much he reveals about the staged rescue is a real ethical decision, not an accidental omission.

**Earlier setup paid off:** The recruitment promise, training bond, jade choice, and arm sacrifice.

**Later event prepared:** An ordinary future with adaptive routines and ongoing relationships.

**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** No cure or substitute arm should undo the permanence established in Chapter 24.

2. **Location:** Departure area, proposed | **Objective:** Mount or prepare to travel | **Resistance:** An ordinary one-handed difficulty | **Outcome:** He accepts help without humiliation.

## Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Proposed: Caide takes an offered hand and mounts the horse, with no need to make the moment heroic.

## One sentence summary

Because the battle has left unfinished hurt between them, Caide apologizes for putting jade ahead of certainty for his crew and begins accepting their help without treating it as failure.
