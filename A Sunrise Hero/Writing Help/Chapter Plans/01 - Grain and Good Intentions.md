**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 1 — Grain and Good Intentions

**POV:** Dan.

**Time and location:** Meroun: an official's office, then Snowdrop Garden; before the worst crop and granary fires.

**Essential characters:** Dan, a grain/storage official (possibly Sung), the princess; Caide optional.

**Approximate word budget:** 3,200 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

## Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Show Dan's administrative talent, sincere loyalty, private resources, and mistaken belief that usefulness will earn sonship.

**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** His later punishment would lack a concrete action to punish and an emotional expectation to betray.

## Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Secure lawful storage or clearance for grain already bought with his own savings.

**Underlying motive:** Protect Meroun and deliver a public success in his father's name.

**Stakes:** His savings, grain spoilage or seizure, and his standing with Cardinale.

## Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Can Dan turn foresight into a workable food reserve?

**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** He obtains a concession, while creating the complaint his father can later weaponize.

## Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** An official resists accepting risk or yielding control over storage.

**What the opposing character wants:** Preserve jurisdiction and avoid responsibility for Dan's speculative purchase.

**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Dan wants to demonstrate competence without admitting how insecure his position is.

## Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** Dan has bought grain but cannot yet store or move it as intended.

**Trigger:** The official threatens to block the arrangement.

**First approach:** Dan presents evidence of an approaching shortage and a practical plan.

**Complication:** The official challenges his authority and common-born standing.

**Response:** Dan uses Cardinale's delegated name and offers Cardinale the public credit.

**Turning point:** The concession is granted, but Dan's unauthorized commitment is recorded or reported.

## Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Wait for explicit permission or commit his father's authority now.

**Cost or risk of each option:** Delay endangers the reserve; proceeding exposes Dan to an accusation of exceeding his mandate.

**Decision:** Proceed, believing a useful result will vindicate him.

**Action taken:** Secure the agreement and later describe his success to his secret lover.

**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** The complaint's consequences arrive in Chapter 3.

## Outcome and change

**Practical result:** The grain plan advances, with a political liability attached.

**Before to after:** Capable representative → capable representative who has overreached.

**Character interpretation:** Dan reads access, pay, and responsibility as evidence that his father values him as a future son.

**Relationship effect:** The princess sees his hope; the official now has grounds to complain.

**Next consequence:** Cardinale can punish the method while benefiting from the result.

## Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** The money is legitimate private earnings; the intended beneficiary of public credit is Cardinale.

**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Dan recognizes his own ingenuity but mistakes usefulness for promised belonging.

**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** The lover's royal identity can remain unrevealed because Dan's scene concerns intimacy and status; avoid conspicuously evasive thoughts.

**Earlier setup paid off:** Establishes the grain investment rather than paying off an earlier scene.

**Later event prepared:** Demotion, depleted cash after Caide's demand, the grain surrender, and the father's later reward.

**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** House of Growth handles agriculture/finance; Cardinale heads Works. Their overlapping interests create jurisdictional friction.

## Scene list

1. **Location:** Official's office | **Objective:** Secure storage or clearance | **Resistance:** Jurisdiction and doubts about Dan's standing | **Outcome:** Agreement obtained with a reportable overreach.
2. **Location:** Snowdrop Garden | **Objective:** Share the success and seek reassurance | **Resistance:** His uncertain future and their unequal status | **Outcome:** Dan recommits to earning his father's recognition.

## Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Dan imagines Cardinale receiving credit and finally seeing him as a son.

## One sentence summary

Because he foresees a shortage, Dan commits his savings to grain and pushes an official into cooperating, gaining a reserve while giving his father grounds to accuse him of overstepping.
