**Chapter number and working title:** Chapter 4 — An Apology and a Burning Ship  
**POV:** Caide.  
**Time and location:** Hira's workshop, the Fool's Mug, and the Crescent River docks; shipment day.  
**Essential characters:** Caide, Hira, Miero, Dan, porters Toku and Matta; Seyla briefly.  
**Approximate word budget:** 4,000 words, provisional; the whole-book allocation is not fixed.

### Purpose

**Main contribution to the book:** Make the promised arm tangible, repair enough trust to continue the friendship, then destroy Caide's immediate hope.  
**What would be lost if this chapter were removed:** The jade loss would lack emotional investment and Caide's coercion would go unacknowledged.

### Character motivation

**Immediate goal:** Confirm the prosthesis, face Dan, and collect the arriving jade.  
**Underlying motive:** Believe his old life is close enough to reclaim.  
**Stakes:** His remaining friendship and the fortune invested in the arm.

### Dramatic question

**Question driving the chapter:** Can Caide put right what he has done and finally move toward restoration?  
**Answer or meaningful progress provided:** Dan offers friendship, but the burning ship destroys the expected practical reward.

### Opposition and tension

**External resistance:** The confession risks rejection; the shipment arrives burning.  
**What the opposing character wants:** Dan wants honesty; the fire is an immediate physical threat with an unknown source.  
**Internal pressure or competing desire:** Caide wants forgiveness without fully accepting that needing help is not shameful.

### Causal sequence

**Starting situation:** Hira demonstrates the prosthesis and Miero admires Caide.  
**Trigger:** Collecting the jade requires Caide to face the friend he coerced.  
**First approach:** He admits arranging the ambush.  
**Complication:** Dan's willingness to help makes the betrayal harder to excuse.  
**Response:** They go to meet the Hundred Moons.  
**Turning point:** The ship burns and sinks before the jade can be secured.

### Decision and action

**Options the character perceives:** Conceal the ambush or admit it; attempt a suicidal retrieval or retreat.  
**Cost or risk of each option:** Honesty may lose Dan; concealment poisons the friendship. Retrieval risks lives for the arm.  
**Decision:** Confess, then leave the burning wreck when recovery is impossible.  
**Action taken:** Accept Dan's response and abandon the immediate salvage attempt.  
**If the chapter breaks before this is completed, where it continues:** Caide's withdrawal leads into Chapter 5.

### Outcome and change

**Practical result:** Some trust returns, but the jade is lost in the river.  
**Before to after:** Anticipating restoration → convinced hope has been taken away again.  
**Character interpretation:** Caide sees himself as trapped in the life he hoped to escape.  
**Relationship effect:** Dan chooses the friendship; Miero and Hira become emotionally important.  
**Next consequence:** Dan will need to give Caide a purpose beyond the prosthesis.

### Reader knowledge and continuity

**What the reader must understand:** Hira makes the arm; jade is the main expense. Remove the ledger-theft and blackmailer explanation.  
**What the POV character knows or misunderstands:** Caide knows he chose coercion; he does not yet know the arm will suppress magic.  
**What remains concealed and why the concealment is fair:** The ship's arsonist can remain unidentified because Caide witnesses the consequence, not its cause.  
**Earlier setup paid off:** The funding and damaged friendship from Chapter 2.  
**Later event prepared:** Hira/Miero's later danger, the new jade opportunity, and optional workshop light-collection setup.  
**Relevant worldbuilding or magic rules:** Inscriptions govern Hira's craft; show a specific demonstration rather than explaining every Word.

### Scene list

3. **Location:** Docks | **Objective:** Collect the jade | **Resistance:** Burning ship and dangerous crowd | **Outcome:** Caide leaves without it.

### Closing beat

**Final action, exchange, image, discovery, or unresolved development:** Caide turns away from the river, unable to turn friendship into hope for himself yet.

### One sentence summary

Because the jade is arriving, Caide faces the friend he coerced and receives forgiveness, only for the burning Hundred Moons to sink his chance of getting the arm.
